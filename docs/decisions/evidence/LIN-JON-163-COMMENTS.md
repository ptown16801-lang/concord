The decision order should remain:

## Comment 6e62d4c6-98e5-48fe-9197-04e3ebfa5346

Date: 2026-09-24T07:07:21.881Z; author: Linear; on behalf of: not stated

The proposal has the right direction, but it is not yet freeze-ready. Its central weakness is that “independence” is asserted as a lineage property without defining the decision rule, evidence threshold, or consequences of incomplete lineage.

* **Authority boundaries:** “Human retains final acceptance” conflicts with “Planner architecture requires qualifying independent review before becoming an implementation contract” unless the human is explicitly prohibited from overriding that gate. Define whether human authority may accept residual risk, and require a recorded exception with scope, rationale, expiry, and accountable human.
* **Normative gaps:** Define MUST/SHOULD/MAY rules; what counts as a contribution; when lineage is unknown; retention and auditability requirements; conflict-of-interest disclosure; evidence freshness; and who can change the framework itself.
* **Lineage weakness:** Provider/model family is too coarse, while session identity is too narrow. Independence must account for shared prompts, context, fine-tuning, tools, memory, orchestration, human operator, repository state, and reuse of another role’s derived artifacts. Unknown or unverifiable lineage must be treated as non-independent for gating purposes.
* **Consequence levels:** A severity scheme is unsafe unless each level has objective triggers and mandatory controls. Define a minimum level for architecture, security assumptions, merge, and release decisions; prohibit self-classification by the contributor; and require escalation where classifications conflict.
* **Roles:** “Security/Governance Reviewer” combines two separable concerns: security assurance and policy/process compliance. Split it only if their evidence standards or escalation paths differ; otherwise retain it but define distinct duties. “Release Steward” needs no authority to deploy—only admission/evidence coordination.
* **Integrator loopholes:** The Integrator must not accept evidence it generated, evidence whose lineage is incomplete, a reviewer’s conclusion without underlying artifacts, or an exception masquerading as equivalent evidence. It must verify required independent evidence against a declared consequence level, reject ambiguous identity/lineage, and record every admission decision.

Smallest changes for final agreement:

1. Add a normative **Independence Decision Rule**: independence requires materially separate provenance across defined dimensions; unknown lineage fails the gate.
2. Add a **Human Exception Rule**: only a named human may override a gate, with written rationale, bounded scope, expiry, and non-transferable accountability.
3. Define **consequence levels** with objective routing, minimum controls, conflict escalation, and prohibition on contributor self-classification.
4. Add an **Integrator Admission checklist** with mandatory rejection conditions and an immutable decision record.
5. Clarify that each role has duties and evidence outputs, but no autonomous authority to change scope, accept risk, merge, or deploy.

Final specification boundary after those changes: a model-neutral governance specification for role responsibilities, evidence lineage, independence determination, consequence-based routing, Integrator admission, and reserved human authority. It excludes implementation, automation behavior, and provider/model qualification
